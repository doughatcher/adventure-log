Rides the Wake carries herself like someone accustomed to moving through rough water — patient in stillness, overwhelming in motion.

She is not reckless in the way people imagine barbarians to be. There is a current of deliberate purpose beneath the fury, a sense that the rage, when it comes, has been earned. Those who travel with her describe a quiet confidence that borders on the serene, right up until it doesn't. The name fits: she goes where the force has been and holds the line in the turbulence that follows.

Shard C took her down. Something called the Grim Reaper — a name that turned out to be a description — found the seams in her endurance and drove through them. Piercing damage, poison, the relentless arithmetic of a creature that wanted her specifically dead. She took the hits that would have broken others. She held. And then, at last, she didn't. The party scrambled — Healing Word bought precious seconds, allies struck back hard — but the Reaper had already made its accounting, and Rides the Wake fell. She died with the kind of defiance that doesn't bother announcing itself. She just kept standing until she couldn't.

Whether the world has seen the last of her is an open question. The name she carries has always implied return — something that goes under, and comes back up. She has been in rough water before.
