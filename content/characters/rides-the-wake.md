Rides the Wake carries herself like someone accustomed to moving through rough water — patient in stillness, overwhelming in motion.

She is not reckless in the way people imagine barbarians to be. There is a current of deliberate purpose beneath the fury, a sense that the rage, when it comes, has been earned. Those who travel with her describe a quiet confidence that borders on the serene, right up until it doesn't. The name fits: she goes where the force has been and holds the line in the turbulence that follows.

Shard C took her down for good. The Grim Reaper — a name that turned out to be a description — had already carved through her once, and came back to finish the accounting. Piercing damage, poison, the cold arithmetic of something that does not tire and does not negotiate. She had taken hits that would have felled others. She had held long enough for the party to draw breath, to strike back, to nearly turn it. A Healing Word bought her seconds. The seconds were not enough.

What she left behind was a shape in the fight that no one else could fill — a wall that had been holding without anyone fully noticing until it was gone. The others said her name afterward in the way people say names when they are still deciding what to do with the grief. Leonard, they called the thing that killed her. A name given to make the loss make sense, to put a face on the indifferent. Rides the Wake would have found that funny.
