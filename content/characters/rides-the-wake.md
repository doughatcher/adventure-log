Rides the Wake carries herself like someone accustomed to moving through rough water — patient in stillness, overwhelming in motion.

She is not reckless in the way people imagine barbarians to be. There is a current of deliberate purpose beneath the fury, a sense that the rage, when it comes, has been earned. Those who travel with her describe a quiet confidence that borders on the serene, right up until it doesn't. The name fits: she goes where the force has been and holds the line in the turbulence that follows.

Shard C took her down for good. The Grim Reaper — a name that turned out to be a description — had already carved through her once, and came back to finish the accounting. Piercing damage, poison, the cold arithmetic of something that does not tire and does not negotiate. She had taken hits that would have felled others. She had held long enough for the party to draw breath, to strike back, to nearly turn it. A Healing Word bought her seconds. The seconds were not enough. The final tally was eleven points of piercing damage against a single hit point remaining — a margin so thin it was almost a joke, and wasn't. She went down swearing, or she went down silent; no one who was there seems to agree. What they agree on is that she was still facing it when she fell.

Leonard — whatever Leonard was, whoever Leonard wore as a face — is the name being passed around as the one that killed her. She would probably find that unbearable. A name like that, attached to an ending like hers. Rides the Wake deserved to be taken by something with the decency not to have a name at all.
