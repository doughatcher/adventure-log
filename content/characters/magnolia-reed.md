The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey occupation of Zobeck: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something. She holds status 7 alongside Aldric, and while his came from a courtroom, hers came from a kitchen. The Shadow Fey court runs on ceremony and hierarchy, but it also runs on feasts. Magnolia understood this.

The Shadow Fey bard who turned her hand against her own party is dead — Judith killed the bard rather than Magnolia. That debt doesn't expire. What it means for how Magnolia moves through the rest of this court, and whether the fey-adjacent instincts that make her fluent here ever put her at odds with the companions who've bled for her, remains to be seen.
