A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that makes him dangerous when he is paying attention.

King Mago did not let the matter rest there. Satisfied enough with the repair to find a use for the man who made it, he appointed Aldric Reader of the Lower Court — a title with teeth, as it turned out. The case of Billoc, a mute goblin accused of theft with his hands bound behind him, was not one most courtiers would have noticed as rigged. Aldric noticed. The party freed Billoc's hands so he could sign, argued that the court had no proof, and walked out with the goblin free and grateful. Aldric came into the palace owing a debt to Mago. He is leaving this session holding court rank of seven and the quiet knowledge that he acquitted himself well when it mattered.
