A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that probably got him into trouble in the first place.

King Mago did not let him leave without extracting something in return. Named Reader of the Lower Court, Aldric found himself presiding over the case of Billoc — a mute goblin, hands bound, accused of theft and functionally incapable of answering the charge in that condition. It was Aldric's party who noticed. Freeing the goblin's hands, allowing him to sign, and demonstrating to Mago that there was no proof — only accusation — was the kind of procedural thinking that cuts through theater and reaches the actual question. Billoc walked free. Aldric's court status sits at seven, which is not where he started this campaign. He earned it by fixing what he broke and then doing the job that came with it.
