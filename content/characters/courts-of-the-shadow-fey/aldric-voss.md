A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that probably caused the problem in the first place.

The court had more use for him. King Mago appointed Aldric Reader of the Lower Court — a title that sounds ceremonial until you are standing in front of a mute goblin accused of theft, hands bound, unable to speak for himself in any language the court will hear. Aldric recognized that the trial was functionally over before it started, and the party moved to change that — freeing Billoc's hands, letting him sign, making the argument that absence of proof is not proof of guilt. Mago accepted it. Billoc walked free. Aldric's status sits at 7, earned through the work of the scholar who also, let the record show, caused most of the trouble that required the scholarship.
