A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that got him into the mess: look at the thing, understand the thing, do something useful with what you've learned.

The title that followed was not ceremonial. King Mago appointed Aldric Reader of the Lower Court, and the first case placed before him was Billoc — a mute goblin with bound hands, accused of theft, incapable of mounting any defense while restrained. Someone with less presence of mind might have read the verdict the court wanted and moved on. Aldric recognized that the process itself was the obstruction, freed Billoc's hands so he could sign, and argued that no proof of theft existed. Mago accepted it. Billoc walked. That is court status earned the hard way — not through flattery or performance, but through doing the actual work correctly when it counted.
