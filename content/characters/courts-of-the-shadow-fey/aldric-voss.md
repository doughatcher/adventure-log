A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that makes him useful: identify the mechanism, fix the mechanism, leave it better than you found it.

What followed was stranger. Mago did not dismiss him after the repair — he installed him. Reader of the Lower Court is a title with edges on all sides, a position of visibility in a place where visibility is both asset and liability. Aldric's first case was Billoc, a mute goblin who could not speak in his own defense because his hands were bound. Aldric noticed this. The party freed the goblin's hands, let him sign, and dismantled the accusation from the ground up. Billoc walked free. The episode says something about what Aldric does when given actual authority: he reads the room for the injustice that is hiding inside the procedure, and he corrects it. That he is now sitting at rank seven — a significant climb from where he started — is a consequence of doing the work rather than performing it.
