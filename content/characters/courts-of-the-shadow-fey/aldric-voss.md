A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that makes him dangerous in the first place.

King Mago did not let the audience end there. Aldric left it with a title: Reader of the Lower Court. Whether this is a reward, a test, or a leash has not yet been determined — possibly all three. His first case was Billoc, a mute goblin with bound hands and no means of defending himself against a theft accusation. Aldric saw the problem clearly: the court's process had made defense structurally impossible. He freed Billoc's hands, let him speak in the only language available to him, and argued successfully that absence of proof is not evidence of guilt. Billoc walked free. The status column moved to seven. Aldric continues to climb on the back of fixing his own mistakes and, occasionally, other people's injustices.
