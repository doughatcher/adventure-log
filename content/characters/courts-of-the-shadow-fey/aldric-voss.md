A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that got him into trouble in the first place.

What followed was more unexpected. King Mago made him Reader of the Lower Court — less an honor than an assignment, the kind of role a court gives someone it wants to observe under pressure. The case before him was Billoc, a mute goblin accused of theft, hands bound, no means of speaking in his own defense. Aldric recognized the problem before anyone else said it aloud. Free the hands, allow the signing, and the accusation collapses for want of proof. Billoc walked free. Aldric is now at status seven, appointed to a function inside the court's own machinery, which is a very different thing from being a visitor. Whether he finds that more useful or more dangerous remains to be seen.
