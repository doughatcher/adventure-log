A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that makes him useful: understand the thing before you decide what to do about it.

King Mago, apparently satisfied, then handed him a title — Reader of the Lower Court — which is either an honor or a trap, and in fey courts these categories overlap more than they should. His first case was Billoc, a mute goblin accused of theft, hands bound, unable to speak in his own defense. Aldric noticed the structural injustice of the thing before he engaged with the legal question of it. The party freed Billoc's hands, let him sign, found no proof, and Billoc walked. That Aldric climbed from deep status debt to court rank seven in a single session is not luck. It is the shape of what happens when a wizard decides that the problem in front of him is worth solving correctly.
