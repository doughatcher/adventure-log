A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian in the guard hall, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech — exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits. Go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned the name of the Black Prince's blue horse, Lazulin, and that the Prince's hunt is a high-court event drawing on quarry conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand. Information gathered not through persuasion rolls and deception but through patience and a piece of meat. Tam noted the dueling system as a possible route upward and has not ruled it out, though he is clearly watching for the angle that does not require bleeding for someone else's entertainment.

He held his own in the guard hall encounter, which began awkwardly and recovered through the group's combined efforts. Tam is at status five alongside Judith — behind the casters, a gap he is aware of and working around. The question is whether he finds his path through the hunt, through the dogs, through Kolya, or through a blade. He has not decided yet, which means he is still looking, which means he is still paying attention.
