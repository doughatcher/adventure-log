A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian in the guard hall, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech — exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits. Go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned the name of the Black Prince's blue horse, Lazulin, and that the hunt is a high-court event with quarry conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand. Intelligence gathered sideways, from the people who care for animals rather than the people who command armies, is still intelligence — and often more reliable.

Tam also scouted dueling culture early in the session, weighing whether combat might be a faster ladder than courtly performance. He decided against it, at least for now, which suggests a pragmatism running underneath the ranger's directness. He is at rank five, lower than the rest of the party, but his gaps are informational rather than personal — he knows where the horses are, who tends them, and what the hunt looks like from the kennel side. That knowledge will matter when the high court's entertainments begin to involve conjured quarry and a prince on a blue horse.
