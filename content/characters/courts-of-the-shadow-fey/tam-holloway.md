A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party probed the guard hall and encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech, which is exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits — go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned that the Black Prince's hunt is a high-court event, that the quarry is conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand, and that the large blue horse named Lazulin belongs to the Prince. None of this is incidental. The hunt is a path upward.

Tam also tested the dueling culture early in the session — measured it, understood that rank can be won or lost in single combat, and apparently decided not to commit to that route yet. He holds status 5, the lowest in the party alongside Judith, which means the pressure to find an angle will only increase as they try to move through the upper court. Whether that angle is the hunt, the dueling ground, or something else, Tam will want it on his own terms.
