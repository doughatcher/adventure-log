A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian in the guard hall, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech — exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits. Go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned the name of the Black Prince's blue horse, Lazulin, and that the Prince's hunt is a high-court event drawing on quarry conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand. Information gathered not through deception or rank, but through the simple credibility of a man the dogs decided to like.

He has also been quietly mapping the dueling culture — testing whether rank can be earned through combat rather than politics, learning the cost of a loss before committing to anything. This is the kind of patience that looks like hesitation but isn't. Tam is building toward something. Whether that something involves the hunt, the Black Prince, or a more direct route through the court's upper levels remains to be seen, but the groundwork is being laid in the language he knows best: trust earned in stables, information traded over kennels, options kept open until the moment they need to close.
