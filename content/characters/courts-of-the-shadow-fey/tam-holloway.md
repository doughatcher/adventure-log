A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian in the guard hall, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech — exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits. Go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned the name of the Black Prince's blue horse, Lazulin, and that the hunt is a high-court event with quarry conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand. Whatever the Black Prince's hunt means for the party's path upward through the court, Tam now knows the stables, the dogs, and the man who keeps them. That kind of knowledge tends to matter at exactly the wrong moment for everyone else.

He also tested the dueling culture, enough to understand the stakes — rank gained or rank lost, no soft landings. He chose not to press the point. A ranger who knows when not to fight is either very wise or very patient, and with Tam the distinction is not always clear.
