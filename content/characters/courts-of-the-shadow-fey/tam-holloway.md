A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian in the guard hall, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech — exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits. Go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned the name of the Black Prince's blue horse, Lazulin, and that the hunt is a high-court event with quarry conjured by Count Sammas Hallowgrin. Information gathered the way Tam gathers most things: sideways, through the animals and the people who care for them, while the court is busy watching itself.

He also tested the dueling culture directly and came away understanding the stakes — rank rises with a win, falls with a loss, and the court treats the whole thing as arithmetic. Tam understands arithmetic. Whether he finds the calculus worth the risk is another question, and his current status of five suggests he is keeping his options open rather than committing to any single path upward.
