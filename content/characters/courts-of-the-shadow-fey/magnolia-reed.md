The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey court: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more visible opportunity. Magnolia did not take that version. The head cook crushed the berries into soup and the lower court took note, and Magnolia sits at status seven because she understood that small promises kept in private are worth more here than grand gestures made in public.

What marks her most is the consistency of it. She is not performing virtue for the court's benefit — she simply does what she said she would do, in a place built entirely on games where people say things they do not mean. That legibility, in this particular environment, is its own kind of power.
