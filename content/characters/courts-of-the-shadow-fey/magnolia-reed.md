The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey occupation of Zobeck: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more visible maneuvers; Magnolia is not the kind of person who lives in that version. She said she would do a thing, she did the thing, and the lower court noticed.

Her court status now sits at seven — tied with Aldric for the highest in the party — and both numbers were earned through similar means: competence applied quietly and consistently, in places others overlooked. That she arrived at seven through soup and kept promises rather than duels or dramatic interventions says something about how she reads this court. The Shadow Fey respond to power in many forms. She has identified which forms are available to her and is using them.
