The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey court: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more visible opportunities, and Magnolia is apparently not the person who tells that version. Status seven. The court has noticed that she means what she says.

What makes her effective here, more than any spell, is that she understands the fey relationship to obligation. A promise kept is not a nicety — it is a demonstration of power, the power of a person whose word holds shape. The Shadow Fey lower court registered this. The path upward is still gated, the upper court still remote, but Magnolia has been accumulating the kind of credit that opens doors without requiring a duel. That approach is slower. It is also, so far, working.
