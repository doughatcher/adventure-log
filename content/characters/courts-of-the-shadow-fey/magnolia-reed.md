The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey court: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more visible opportunities. Magnolia did not take that version. A promise made to the people at the bottom of a hierarchy is still a promise, and honoring it in a place built on fey bargains and conditional loyalties is, in its own way, a political act.

She sits at rank seven, tied with Aldric for the highest standing in the party. That number was not purchased through spectacle. It was accumulated through attention — to obligations, to the right leverage points, to the parts of a power structure that other people walk past because they are looking for the throne room and not the kitchen.
