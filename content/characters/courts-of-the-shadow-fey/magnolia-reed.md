The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey court: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more visible opportunities. Magnolia did not take that version. A druid who honors a promise made to cooks, in a court full of nobles angling for the next rung — that is a specific kind of character, and it is working. Her status sits at seven, tied for highest in the party, built almost entirely from competence and reliability in places others overlooked.
