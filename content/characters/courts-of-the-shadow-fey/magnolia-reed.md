The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey court: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more visible opportunities, and Magnolia is simply not that person. She made the promise, she had the magic, she did the thing. The lower court noticed.

That she and Aldric both sit at status seven — the highest in the party — is not a coincidence. Both of them have found ways to work within the court's own logic rather than against it. Magnolia's path has been quieter: build relationships in the places power overlooks, honor small obligations, let the goodwill accumulate. In a palace run on favors and appearances, she understands that the kitchens feed everyone, including the people who decide who rises.
