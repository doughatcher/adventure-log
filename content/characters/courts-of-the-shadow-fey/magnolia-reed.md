The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey court: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more visible opportunity. Magnolia did not tell that version. She showed up with the berries.

That consistency has earned her the highest status in the party alongside Aldric — seven, achieved not through combat or courtly performance but through attentiveness to the people the court considers beneath notice. Cooks. Mute goblins. The unglamorous infrastructure of a glamorous place. It is possible that Magnolia understands fey courts better than she admits, or better than the court expects her to.
