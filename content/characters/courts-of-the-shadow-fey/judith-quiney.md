The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from the edge of awkward. Twenty-four on the follow-through. That is a particular skill: not the skill of being flawless, but the skill of knowing when you are losing and changing the game before the loss becomes permanent.

She claimed the party as a platoon representing the free city of Zobek — a city the guards had not heard of, which in this context is an asset rather than a liability. Judith understands instinctively that unfamiliarity is a surface you can write on. She holds status at five, earned through recovery as much as performance, which is probably exactly how she would want it recorded.
