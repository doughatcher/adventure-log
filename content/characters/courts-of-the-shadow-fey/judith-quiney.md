The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from the edge of awkward. Twenty-four on the check. The party arrived as representatives of Zobeck, a free city the Shadow Fey are apparently interested in folding into their territory, which is exactly the kind of story that sounds plausible because it contains enough truth to stand on.

Status five, same as Tam, which understates what she contributed. The guard hall interaction recovered because of her, and it recovered cleanly — no blood, no escalation, no wasted capital. ShankSpeare has not yet needed the first part of that name inside these walls. She seems patient about it.
