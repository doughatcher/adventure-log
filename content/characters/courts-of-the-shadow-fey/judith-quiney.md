The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards ended the exchange impressed enough. Status: five.

There is something telling in the sequence. The performance failed; the recovery succeeded. Judith is not someone who needs the first move to work — she needs the last move to work, and she has enough self-possession to make that distinction in real time. Whether that quality will carry her higher in a court where rank is tracked and every interaction is a potential transaction remains to be seen. She is paying attention. She is always paying attention.
