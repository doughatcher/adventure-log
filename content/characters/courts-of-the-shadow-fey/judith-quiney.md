The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards ended the conversation satisfied. That recovery — from a fumbled performance to a clean twenty-four on deception — is the kind of thing that looks effortless and is not.

She sits at status five alongside Tam, behind Magnolia and Aldric, which is the sort of gap that a swashbuckler notices and files away. Judith operates best when she has a clear target and enough room to maneuver, and the lower court has been, so far, mostly a problem of navigation rather than confrontation. That may change. The upper court is above them, access is gated by rank, and Judith has already demonstrated that she will find a way through a locked door — the only question is which tool fits the lock.
