The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards walked away believing they had met someone worth not dismissing.

There is a pattern here: Judith recovers. The initial play doesn't always land, but the adjustment usually does, and the adjustment tends to be sharper than the opening move suggested she was capable of. Status 5, same as Tam, still below Aldric and Magnolia — but the gap between 5 and 7 in this court is not a wall. It is a door, and Judith has demonstrated she can find the lock even when she arrives at it from the wrong direction.
