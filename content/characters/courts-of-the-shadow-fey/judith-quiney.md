The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards ended the conversation satisfied. Judith ended it at status five with the quiet satisfaction of someone who recovered a bad roll through nerve rather than luck.

What the guard hall showed, more than anything, is that Judith is genuinely adaptable — not in the way that means she has no commitments, but in the way that means she does not let pride in a particular approach get in the way of the outcome she actually wants. The swords are clean because she maintains them, not because she hasn't used them. She'll find the right moment to make that point somewhere more useful than a guard station.
