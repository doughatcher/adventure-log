The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards ended the conversation satisfied. Her status holds at five, which tells you something about the court's metrics, but says nothing useful about Judith's actual utility to this party.

What the guard hall encounter shows is the particular competence of someone who fails in public without losing composure. The bad roll happened, the insult about clean swords happened, and then she threaded a twenty-four through the needle and walked out with what the party needed. That is a swashbuckler's skill set used correctly: not the absence of difficulty, but the recovery from it.
