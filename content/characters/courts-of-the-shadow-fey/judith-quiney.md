The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades and no blood are not impressive to soldiers who've seen actual combat. She recovered with a deception check that hit hard enough to pull the encounter back from awkward, and the guards walked away believing they'd met someone worth not dismissing. Status 5, same as Tam, earned in a room full of people who were not inclined to give it.

Swashbucklers live in the space between the social and the lethal — the Charisma investment is real, the blade is real, and the art is in knowing which one the moment calls for. Judith is still finding her footing in a court where performance is the point and every room is an audience. That may suit her better than she's letting on.
