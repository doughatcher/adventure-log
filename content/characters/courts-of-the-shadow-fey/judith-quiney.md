The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards ended the conversation mildly impressed. Status five.

What the guard hall showed is that Judith is most dangerous not when the first plan works, but when it doesn't. A rogue who can only operate when conditions are ideal is a liability in a court like this one, where conditions are never ideal and the social terrain shifts without announcement. Judith found the seam, slid through it, and walked out with something to show for it. The gap between five and seven is real, but she is not the kind of person who panics about a scoreboard. She is the kind of person who waits for the right moment to close it.
