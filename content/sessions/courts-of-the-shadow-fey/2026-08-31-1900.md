The lower court of the Shadow Fey palace breathed with the particular industry of a place that keeps great things running without being permitted to witness them. Stone corridors carried the smell of strange cookfire and tallow, and the distant music of the upper courts fell here as little more than a tremor in the walls. The party had learned quickly enough that in this place, every errand was an audition and every promise a debt that would eventually be called.

Magnolia had made hers to the kitchens, and she paid it without complaint. Fifty goodberries, conjured from what little green life her magic could coax into a realm of perpetual shadow, were presented to the head cook, who received them with the gravity of someone accepting coin. The berries were crushed into soup before the afternoon was out, and the smell that rose from the pots was warm and strange and wholly out of place beneath the palace's cold vaulting. Word moved, as word does in servants' quarters, and Magnolia's standing moved with it.

Aldric's day was less quiet. He was summoned before King Mago over the wardstone he had shattered with his own fireball on the Shadow Road — an incident that had very nearly been his last. The stone, he now understood, had functioned as something between an alarm and a letter: a ward that listened and reported. He examined it in the presence of Ten-Pound Hammer, the goblin blacksmith who had inspected the damage, and then he worked. When the magic settled, the stone was whole and somewhat better than it had been. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the repair with a nod that communicated professional satisfaction and nothing warmer. King Mago, however, was not finished with Aldric. He named him Reader of the Lower Court before the day was done, an office that sounded ceremonial until the first case was brought forward.

The case was Billoc's — a goblin, mute from birth, whose only tongue was the movement of his hands. He stood accused of theft and could say nothing in his own defense, because his hands had been bound behind him when he was brought in. Aldric saw it before the others could frame the argument: a man who can only speak with his hands cannot speak at all with his hands bound, and a court that silences the accused before they can answer is not hearing a case at all. The party made the point plainly before King Mago, and Mago, to his credit, listened. There was no proof of theft. Billoc was freed. He found Aldric afterward and said what he could without words, which was enough.

Tam went to the stables, where he was more comfortable than he would ever be in a throne room. The Black Prince's hunting dogs were kept there under the care of Kolya, the kennel master, a figure who measured people by how his animals received them. Tam arrived with meat and the capacity to speak to creatures in their own terms, and the dogs made their judgment quickly. Kolya thawed by degrees. Over the course of the afternoon Tam learned the shape of what waited above them: the Black Prince's hunt was a high court affair, the quarry conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand's summoning arts, and the great blue horse Lazulin carried the Prince himself. An invitation to the hunt would be worth considerably more than a repaired wardstone.

Judith drifted toward the guard hall, where soldiers from Allosis stood their rotations under Captain Memorethian — Shadow Fey far from their own holdings, posted here as the palace's martial backbone. She made herself known and tried the old soldier's trick of suggesting common cause between fighters. The captain's people were not unfriendly, but they were not forthcoming either. They slept in the hall, walked the walls, sharpened their swords, and kept their own counsel. Judith learned that the walls existed and that the soldiers walked them, which was less than she had hoped and more than nothing.

When the candles burned low and the lower court settled into its particular variety of restless quiet, the four of them compared what they had gathered. Status had moved — Aldric and Magnolia had climbed to seven, Tam and Judith to five — but the upper courts remained as remote as they had been at dawn. The hunt was the clearest rope dangling within reach: Kolya liked Tam, Lazulin waited in his stall, and somewhere above them Count Samus Hollowgrand conjured things for powerful men to chase. Billoc was loose in the palace corridors, grateful and invisible, which was perhaps worth more than it seemed. The Summer Queen's season was still turning toward its end, and the cold that came after would not wait for them to feel ready.
