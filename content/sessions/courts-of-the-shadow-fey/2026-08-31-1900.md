The lower court of the Shadow Fey palace smelled of tallow smoke and damp stone, of old debts and older grudges, and the party moved through it with the careful patience of people who had learned, finally, that rank here was something earned rather than seized. Their first full day in service had not broken them. It had, instead, begun to shape them into something the court could recognize.

Magnolia Reed kept her word to the kitchens, and in the Shadow Realm, a kept promise is a coin of real weight. She had pledged goodberries, and she delivered them — fifty of them, bright and verdant as anything that had no business existing in this lightless place. The head cook received them with the solemnity of a priest accepting an offering and crushed them without ceremony into a great pot of soup. The court ate. Magnolia's status rose. There was a lesson in it, if one was paying attention: the palace's stomach remembered those who fed it.

Aldric's reckoning came more formally. King Mago summoned him to answer for the wardstone destroyed by his fireball on the Shadow Road — that desperate, killing moment that had cost him his own life and left a scar in the realm's warding fabric. Brought before the goblin king, Aldric examined the damaged stone with a diviner's eye, understood what it had been — a message-ward, an alarm woven into the infrastructure of the road itself — and then, rather than offering apology, offered repair. His magic was careful and precise, and when Ten-Pound Hammer inspected the work, the blacksmith found it sound. The debt was settled. King Mago, apparently satisfied that a wizard who could break a thing and then improve it was more useful than one who merely broke things, made Aldric his Reader of the Lower Court before the day was out.

The appointment was tested almost immediately. A mute goblin named Billoc stood accused of theft, his hands bound behind him, unable to make any gesture in his own defense. The court might have processed the case without pause — Billoc had no voice, no advocate, no apparent means of rebuttal. But Aldric, and those watching with him, recognized the injustice built into the proceedings: the only language the prisoner possessed was the movement of his hands, and those hands were bound. They pressed the case before Mago with enough clarity that the king could not easily dismiss it. Billoc's hands were freed, the signs he made were read, and no proof of theft could be produced to hold him. He walked free, and the look he gave the party as he went carried something that would need no translation in any tongue.

While Aldric navigated the court's formal channels, Tam Holloway found his own angle in the stables. The Black Prince's hunting hounds were large and suspicious and entirely susceptible to both raw meat and a ranger who could speak the simple languages of beasts. Tam fed them, and they remembered him, and in their company he met Kolya, the kennel master, who proved willing enough to talk. From Kolya, Tam learned the shape of something useful: the Black Prince's hunt was a high-court affair, the quarry conjured to order by a summoner named Count Samus Hollowgrand, the Black Prince himself riding Lazulin, his enormous blue horse. A path from the stables upward into the hunt's company might exist, if the party could find the right footing.

Judith, for her part, walked into the guard hall where soldiers from Allosis stood watch under Captain Memorethian, and found a room full of fey in arms who were comfortable in their posting and not inclined to share much with strangers from a city they had barely heard of. She gave them a name — Zobeck, the free city — and the soldiers received it with the mild interest of men who had been told that somewhere in the distance a satellite was being added to the empire's collection. She left with a slightly better sense of the hall's layout than she had entered with, and little else. Some doors, it seemed, were not yet ready to open.

By the time the torches in the lower corridors had burned low toward their nightly guttering, the shape of what the party had accomplished began to settle. Two of them had climbed to the seventh rank of the lower court; two stood at the fifth. Billoc was somewhere in the palace, free and grateful, which was a resource not yet used. Kolya's goodwill sat in the stables, and the dogs would know Tam's scent the next time he came. Above them, through stone and courtly protocol and the slow machinery of fey politics, the Summer Queen's court continued its session — but the season was turning, and the clock that had no face on it kept running, indifferent to how much the party still needed to do.
