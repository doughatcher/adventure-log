The road to the Winter Palace had turned them away before — seven days prior, the same cold iron gate, the same implacable guardian, and the humiliation of being sent back down the slick cobblestones with nothing to show for it. This time the party climbed that treacherous passage with more care and, in at least one case, considerably more dignity. The footing was slippery enough that each of them had to earn their way up, but the fourteen steps to Executioner's Gate were navigated without anyone tumbling back into the shadow-lit streets below.

The gate itself was a grim welcome. Oak banded with iron, shut against the night, its flanking walls hung with the shriveled heads and ironbound corpses of those who had crossed the court's patience too far. The remains were years old and showed it, but the fey kept them there precisely for that reason — a silent argument about who held dominion here, and what it cost to forget it. Standing before all of it was the monolith footman, the same animated suit of enchanted armor that had turned them away without ceremony the week before. It regarded the approaching party with the blank patience of something that had never once considered mercy.

Aldric stepped forward. He produced the dagger with the raven insignia — the one that had opened other doors in this strange realm — and offered the footman a greeting with more warmth than the situation perhaps warranted. Good to see you, he said, as though they were old acquaintances. We wish to pass. We have invitations. There was a breath of silence in which the iron sentinel could have done anything at all. Then it bowed, and stood aside.

The others filed through behind him, Judith noting quietly that her status entitled her retainers to follow, and the footman made no objection. The party crossed the threshold one by one, the puppy — Kolya's runt, destined for the young Princess Vailessia — apparently counting as a member of the delegation in good standing. The gate did not close against them. The guardian returned to its post without another word.

Ahead lay the Winter Palace in full: its corridors of shadow-cold stone, its unresolved threats, its waiting secrets. Vaelith Graveshade's unspoken confession still hung in the air somewhere behind them. Lady Sariel remained a rumor with a name. Count Hallowgrin had yet to meet their eyes, and the angel Revich kept his vigil in the Tower of Light, all-seeing, untouched, unmoved. But for the first time, the party stood on the right side of the gate to do anything about any of it. The Winter Palace had let them in, and the work — the real work — could finally begin.
