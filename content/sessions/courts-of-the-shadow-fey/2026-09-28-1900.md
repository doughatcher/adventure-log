The cobblestone passage leading up to Executioner's Gate was slick with the particular damp that the Shadow Realm seemed to breathe out of the stone itself, and the party picked their way upward with varying degrees of grace. Shriveled heads and iron-caged remains lined the palace wall on either side of the gate's great oak-and-iron doors, years old but preserved by some cold fey working — not trophies so much as arguments, silent and absolute, about who held dominion here. The footman standing before those doors was no less unsettling for being familiar. It was the same monolith soldier who had turned them away a week prior, enchanted plate and wicked blade and no discernible patience for uncertainty.

The party conferred in hushed tones before the final approach, settling on an order. Judith's standing at court made her the safest face to lead with, but it was Aldric who stepped forward first, the raven-insignia dagger drawn and presented not as a threat but as a credential. He offered a greeting that was almost warm — the studied ease of a man who had spent recent days learning what currency looked like in this court. Good to see you, he said, more or less, and announced that they wished to pass.

The footman regarded them. Whatever calculation ran behind that enchanted stillness, it ran quickly. The dagger was seen, the party's bearing was assessed, and then the soldier bowed — a small, precise thing — and stepped aside. Aldric kept walking without changing his pace, and the others followed, each managing their relief in their own way. Tam brought up the rear, checking over his shoulder once before the gate fell behind them. The puppy, tucked along for Princess Vailessia, was apparently unbothered by any of it.

They were inside the Winter Palace. After weeks of climbing the lower court's slow social ladder — wardstones repaired, goblins defended, favors extended through infirmaries and kennels and dueling rings — the doors had simply opened. Whether it was the dagger, the court standing they had accumulated, or the particular dignity of arriving with a dog on an errand for an eight-year-old ward of the court, none of them could have said for certain. Perhaps all three. The Shadow Fey had a way of making the complicated feel deceptively simple at the moment it resolved, which was itself a reason to remain careful.

The threads left dangling behind them were not forgotten, only deferred. Vaelith Graveshade's secret still wanted for an answer. Lady Sariel remained a moody, half-rumored presence somewhere deeper in the palace, her trouble with Count Hallowgrin unresolved. And above it all, in the Tower of Light, an all-seeing angel called Revich reportedly watched everything. The party had come a long way to stand on this side of Executioner's Gate. What the Winter Palace intended to make of their arrival remained, as with most things in the Shadow Realm, entirely unclear.
