The slick cobblestones of the passage rose steeply toward the palace wall, and the climb demanded more care than pride permitted. One by one the party picked their way upward through the treacherous dark, boots finding uncertain purchase on stones slicked by something that was not quite ice and not quite dew — some property of the Shadow Realm that made every surface feel like a suggestion. They reached the landing intact, and there before them stood Executioner's Gate.

It deserved its name. The wall to either side was lined with the shriveled heads of enemies past and the iron-caged remains of those who had been made examples. The fey kept the dead in place by decree rather than by decay, and the effect was precisely what was intended: a reminder, patient and absolute, of where all defiance ended. The gate itself was heavy oak banded with iron, shut against the night, and in front of it stood the monolith footman — the same figure of enchanted armor that had turned the party away seven days before, wicked blade at its side, presence like a locked door given form.

It was Aldric who walked forward. He reached into his coat and produced the dagger bearing the raven insignia, holding it where the footman could see, and offered a greeting that managed to be both respectful and entirely without apology. The footman regarded them. The seconds stretched. Then it bowed, stepped aside, and said nothing further. Aldric nodded his thanks and kept walking. Judith followed, offering a pleasant word to the armored sentinel on her way past. Tam and Magnolia came after, and the party crossed the threshold of the Winter Palace without incident, the puppy trotting along at their heels, blissfully unaware that it had just been smuggled past one of the most forbidding checkpoints in the Shadow Realm.

The gate closed behind them, and the footman returned to its post as though nothing of consequence had occurred — which, from its perspective, was probably accurate. What mattered was that the party was inside. The long weeks of careful maneuvering through the lower court, the wardstone repair, the dueling season, the goblin messenger's errand, the runt of the litter selected with such deliberation from Kolya's kennels — all of it had been currency spent toward this single threshold, and at last the debt had been collected.

Somewhere above them, Princess Vailessia was waiting for her puppy. And somewhere further still, deeper in the palace's cold and luminous geometry, lay the Tower of Light where Revich was said to dwell, and the chambers of Count Hallowgrin, and the brooding mystery of Lady Sariel, and the court's countless other unsettled accounts. The party had earned their entry. What they would find on the other side of it remained entirely unwritten.
