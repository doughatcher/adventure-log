The road up to Executioner's Gate had done the party no favors. The cobblestones were glazed with something between frost and shadow-damp, and each member of the company made the ascent with the careful dignity of people unwilling to fall on their faces before an audience of shriveled heads. The gate itself was iron-banded oak, shut fast against the night, and the wall on either side bore its grim decorations with the patience of old stone — corpses in iron cages, skulls darkened by years of fey weather, the whole display arranged less as a warning than as a statement of settled fact. The Winter Palace did not need to threaten. It simply reminded.

The monolith footman standing at the gate was the same construct that had turned them away seven days prior, and it showed no sign of embarrassment at the reunion. Aldric stepped forward before anyone could deliberate further. He produced the dagger bearing the raven insignia and offered the footman a greeting with more warmth than the occasion strictly required — good to see you, we wish to pass, we have invitations — the sort of cheerful confidence that either works completely or makes everything considerably worse. The footman regarded him with the impassivity of enchanted armor.

Then it bowed, and stood aside.

Aldric kept walking, one eye tracking his companions, his expression carefully composed into something that did not look like relief. Judith fell in behind him with the practiced ease of someone accustomed to moving through doors she was not entirely certain would open. The others followed. There had been some small debate, on the way up, about the order of approach — court rank, social standing, who projected the most legitimate authority — but in the end the footman had only cared about the dagger and the greeting, and none of that deliberation had mattered at all.

The dog came through as well, the runt of Kolya's litter bound for Princess Vailessia. Someone told it that it had done a good job.

And then the gate was behind them, and the Winter Palace was ahead. After weeks of negotiation and indignity and one spectacular death and revival on the Shadow Road, after wardstones and duel rings and goblin courts and the long work of climbing a status ladder one careful rung at a time, they had arrived. The air inside smelled of cold stone and old candle wax and something faintly floral that did not belong to any season the material world would recognize. Whatever came next, they were through.
