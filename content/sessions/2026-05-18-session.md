The darkness came for Rides the Wake not in a grand collision of fate but in the quiet accumulation of wounds — the kind of ending that arrives before anyone is ready to acknowledge it as such. The battle had been grinding, relentless, the sort of fight where every victory bought only a moment's breath before something else pressed forward. Granit had gone to his knees at least once, calling on whatever reserve of divine stubbornness keeps a Life Cleric standing when the arithmetic of injury has long since ceased to favor it. Healing Word had been spent like coin down a drain, each casting buying seconds rather than safety. Vargr's voice — that strange, spirit-threaded instrument that had carried them through corrupted groves and flooded shrines — was stretched thin, his Bardic Inspiration given out to hands that needed it more than he did.

It was Rides the Wake who drew the killing attention. She had struck recklessly — her nature, her gift, the thing that made her devastating and dangerous in equal measure — and the enemy recognized the invitation. The blow that followed was precise in the way that suggested something beyond mere combat instinct: twenty-four to hit, the piercing damage halved but followed immediately by poison threading through the wound, eleven points in total. She had perhaps twenty-five left when it struck. Then came the second. The Barbarian who had stood at the front of every terrible thing this party had walked into went still.

The others fought on because that is what you do when someone falls — you make sure they did not fall for nothing. Granit called out for death saving throws, his voice carrying the particular weight of a healer who has learned not to let grief into their hands while those hands are still needed. Lyvriele moved with her characteristic economy of motion, Fey Step carrying her where the geometry of the fight demanded, attacks landing with the precision of someone who cannot afford to miss. Vargr cast Starry Wisp into the melee and watched one of their tormentors go down under its cold light, the Goliath muttering something that might have been a dedication to the spirit or might have been a curse — the difference, in his practice, was often a matter of tone.

One of the enemies, bleeding out and furious, made the kind of promise that dying things make when they want to take something with them. "I'm going down and I'm taking you with me," it spat at Granit, and it meant it — a final full assault, twenty-four to hit, nine more points of piercing damage. Granit absorbed it and held, which said something about dwarven constitutions and probably more about spite. The thing called Leonard — and someone would remember that name, someone always does — had apparently taken credit for the kill. The party would remember that too.

When the last enemy was down and the silence settled over them like sediment, they took stock of what remained. The inventory needed checking; supplies had been burned through faster than anyone had planned. Rides the Wake's condition sat at the edge of what any of them wanted to examine too closely, the numbers on her sheet carrying a weight that transcended arithmetic. Someone mentioned the other islands — that they didn't know enough about them, that knowledge of what lay ahead in the Sea of Governance had become urgent in a way it hadn't been an hour ago. The rebels they'd made contact with might have answers, if the party could reach them before whatever hired Cael Duren decided to make a second approach.

Above it all, the shape of what was coming loomed in ways that resisted clean description. The Compass of Lost Tides had shown them the leylines, the dark miasma spreading through the world's connective tissue. Whatever governed this Shardsea — whatever institutional or political power had made it what it was — was part of that larger pattern, and the pattern did not wait for the party to rest. They had next targets to determine, a character's status to reckon with honestly, and somewhere in the city's lower architecture, people who called themselves rebels and had not yet been given reason to regret trusting strangers who arrived bloody and alive.
