The creature that loomed over them at session's end was not merely powerful — it was the Grim Reaper itself, a being that seemed to draw the very light inward and swallow it whole. The party had faced ancient elementals, corrupted ocean spirits, and poisoned steel in dark alleys, but nothing had worn quite this shape before: death given form and intention, patient as only something that always wins can afford to be.

The fight was brutal from the first exchange. Granit braced and held as the party's anchor, divine power cycling through his hands toward those who needed it most. Vargr called out across the din — *Ryan is fucked up, Rides the Wake is fucked up* — and spent his bonus action casting Healing Word, threading restoration through the chaos like a rope thrown into rough water. It bought seconds. Seconds were all they had. Lyvriele moved with the cold precision that had carried her through two shardseas already, her Starry Wisp streaking out and striking true, and when one of the lesser combatants went down she did not celebrate — there was still the larger shadow.

Rides the Wake met the Reaper head-on the way she met everything: patient in stillness, then overwhelming. The Reaper hit back with a twenty-four. Nine points of piercing damage halved by her rage, then seven points of poison threading through the wound — eleven total, and though she had faced worse on paper, this was different. Something in the nature of the blow carried a weight that simple numbers could not account for. Her hit points gave out. The fury that had carried her across burning mountains and storm-wracked reefs went quiet, and Rides the Wake fell.

Granit called for death saving throws. The party fought on around her still form, Vargr and Lyvriele pressing the creature while the dwarf cleric kept one eye on his fallen companion and one hand ready. The Reaper struck at Granit next — *going down and taking you with me* — but the party did not break. There was a grim, wordless language between them by now, earned across the Shardsea of Elements and Nature's Wrath alike, and it held even here. Eighteen damage landed on the creature from an answered strike, and the tide, if it could be called that, shifted by the narrowest of margins.

When the last blow fell and the room grew still, the survivors stood among the wreckage of the encounter and began the grim arithmetic of who remained and at what cost. Someone spoke a name — Leonard — with the half-laughing despair of the still-living. The inventory would need checking. The wounds would need accounting. And somewhere beyond this room, other islands waited, other islands that knew nothing yet of what had transpired here, where the Grim Reaper had loomed over four adventurers and only three had risen.

Rides the Wake did not.
