The harbor of the Governance Sea smelled of salt and old authority — tar-black rope, the iron tang of chain, the particular stillness of a city that has learned to watch strangers arrive without letting its face change. The party made landfall in the last of the day's light, their ship settling against Pier Seven with the weary confidence of a vessel that had earned its berth. Whatever peace the crossing had afforded them lasted approximately as long as it took to tie off the lines.

A man was waiting. Cael Duren had the easy posture of someone accustomed to being the most dangerous thing in a room and the intelligence not to say so. He stood with two companions whose names he did not offer, and he spoke in the measured cadence of a man delivering a message he had rehearsed: someone wanted what was in their hold. He named no patron. He made no move. The threat was professional — wrapped in civility the way a blade is wrapped in cloth, still a blade. Vargr let music answer where words might have escalated, something low and unsettling that pressed against the silence of the pier and found the seams. Duren's jaw tightened. He held. He left. The party watched him go and knew they had not seen the last of the thing he represented.

The lower city swallowed them in narrow streets and the smell of tallow fires. They were looking for rebels — had caught wind of their existence through whatever thread of rumor and necessity had brought the party this far into the Governance Sea's politics. What found them first was three men with poisoned daggers and the economical violence of professionals. The ambush was nearly decisive. Granit went down, then Rides the Wake, then Vargr — one by one, the pillars of the party's endurance cracking under coordinated pressure. Lyvriele Enafrya did not fall. She moved through the chaos with the cold precision of someone who had made her peace with what she was, and when she was finished the bandits were not a problem anymore.

The aftermath had the particular quality of survival that has not yet decided what it means. Three bodies cooling on stone. Four adventurers taking inventory of damage that a night's rest might not fully answer. And somewhere ahead in the tangle of the lower streets, the rebels — whoever they were, whatever they wanted — apparently willing to offer something that resembled contact. The Governance Sea had announced itself plainly enough: the dangers here did not erupt from volcanoes or howl from corrupted forests. They waited at docks. They wore reasonable faces. They paid men to use poison, and they let the silence after the attack do the rest of the work.

The party moved on. Behind them, the city watched with the patient attention of institutions that have learned how long to wait.
