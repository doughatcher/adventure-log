The harbor smelled of salt and bureaucracy in equal measure — tar-sealed ledgers stacked beside coiled rope, harbormaster's seals pressed into wax next to cargo manifests that had outlasted the merchants who filed them. The party's ship settled into its berth at the edge of the Shardsea of Governance with the quiet authority of a vessel that had survived worse waters, and for a long moment no one moved to disembark. There was something in the air here that was neither welcoming nor hostile — only watchful, the way institutions are watchful, noting your arrival, filing it somewhere, waiting to see what category you would eventually prove yourself to occupy.

Rides the Wake was the first down the gangplank, as she usually was, her boots finding the dock planks with the automatic ease of someone who has long since stopped distinguishing between ship-deck and solid ground. Granit followed, heavy and unhurried, taking in the architecture of the port with the slow assessment of someone who knows that what a building is made of tells you what the people inside are made of. Lyvriele paused at the rail a moment longer than the others, committing the layout to memory — the sight lines, the chokepoints, the faces that turned and then deliberately turned away. Vargr came last, already listening to something the rest of them couldn't quite hear, fingers brushing the worn edge of his spiritual focus as though the spirits of this place were whispering in a dialect he was only beginning to parse.

What they found was not the dangerous beauty of the Shardsea of Elements, nor the creeping wrongness of Nature's Wrath. Governance was dangerous the way a contract is dangerous — in the fine print, in the clauses that only become visible when someone invokes them. Ships moved through the harbor with the choreography of long habit, each one knowing its lane, its priority, its place in an order that had been established long before any of this crew was born. Power here was not erupting from a volcano or bleeding through from the Feywild. It was sitting behind a desk somewhere, reviewing documents, deciding who would be permitted to proceed and who would find their passage suddenly, inexplicably, delayed.

They had rations to purchase and charts to acquire — the practical arithmetic of survival that follows every arrival — but underneath those immediate concerns was the larger shape of what this Shardsea would demand of them. The pattern was becoming legible: raw force, then corruption of essence, and now the corruption of structure, of institution, of the systems that civilizations build to hold themselves together. Whatever had gone wrong in the Shardsea of Governance, it would not announce itself with storms or feral spirits. It would look, at first, like paperwork. It would look like policy. It would look, perhaps, entirely normal, right up until the moment it did not.

The harbor bell rang the hour, and somewhere in the city beyond the docks, decisions were being made that the party did not yet know would matter.
