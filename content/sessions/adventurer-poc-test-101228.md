The harbor smelled of salt and bureaucracy — tar and old rope and the particular staleness of forms filled in triplicate. The party made berth in the Shardsea of Governance as the afternoon light turned the water the color of old brass, and for a moment there was nothing asked of them but to stand on solid ground and remember what it felt like.

It was, by any measure, a quiet arrival. No erupting volcano greeted them, no corrupted spirit rose from the tideline to demand their lives as payment for passage. The dockworkers moved with the practiced indifference of people who had seen too many strange ships to be impressed by one more, and the administrative machinery of the port ground on around them like a millstone that had long since forgotten what it was grinding. Rides the Wake watched it all with the patience of someone who has learned that stillness before motion is not weakness. Granit simply waited, solid as their name, reading the place the way a healer reads a patient — not for what it says, but for what it won't.

Lyvriele catalogued the port with quiet Eladrin precision: the placement of naval vessels, the weight of authority in the harbor master's posture, the way coin changed hands slightly too quickly near the eastern moorings. Vargr, for his part, listened to what the place remembered. Old ports remember everything — the names of ships that never came home, the agreements made in harbormaster offices that rewrote the fates of islands. He filed it away. The spirits here were not restless yet, but the Shardsea of Governance was a place where the dead had often died over paperwork, and that kind of resentment lingers.

There were provisions to secure, charts to find, and the deeper question none of them had yet put into words: what, precisely, had gone wrong here? The pattern held — nature's excess, then corruption's rot, now something colder and more deliberate. Civilization's nervous system, and somewhere within it, a lesion. The Compass of Lost Tides rested quiet in its housing, waiting. The party had learned by now that the quiet never lasted. They used it while they had it, and they resupplied, and they listened, and they prepared to find out what governance looked like when it broke.
