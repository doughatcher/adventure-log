The harbors of the Shardsea of Governance smelled of salt, ink, and old money. After the raw violence of corrupted nature and screaming elementals, the docks here felt almost civilized — which was, as any seasoned traveler knew, precisely when one ought to be most careful. Rides the Wake, Granit, Lyvriele, and Vargr stepped off their vessel into a world where power wore a different face: ledgers instead of claws, jurisdictions instead of fangs, and bureaucratic authority sharp enough to draw blood without ever raising a voice.

They had arrived whole, rested, and provisioned — or nearly so. The ship needed charts for waters none of them had sailed before, and the Shardsea of Governance was not the kind of place where one wandered without proper documentation. Ration stores required replenishing before the next leg of any journey. Small concerns, the sort that accumulate when the world gives you a moment to breathe between catastrophes. The party moved through the port district taking stock, their sea legs steadying beneath them as they read the rhythms of a place organized not around survival but around control.

No great enemy had yet revealed itself. No corruption oozed from the stones, no elemental fury bent the horizon. But the Shardsea of Governance had a way of making itself known through texture rather than spectacle — in the way harbor officials watched vessels arrive, in the layered seals on the dockmaster's manifests, in the quiet negotiations happening just beyond earshot in chandlers' back rooms. Vargr had already begun cataloguing the shape of the place, the way he always did, tracing the outlines of something he could not yet name. The dead here would have different things to say than the dead of Tidecross or Glaciarra.

For now, it was enough to be standing on solid ground in a new sea, alive, and paying attention. Whatever Governance had to teach them — or demand of them — had not yet shown its hand.
