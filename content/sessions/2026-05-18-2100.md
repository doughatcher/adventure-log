The session's memory begins in motion — blades already drawn, bodies already bleeding. Whatever calm the party had carried into the Shardsea of Governance had long since been spent, and what remained was the ugly arithmetic of a fight stretching past its welcome. Rides the Wake took the worst of it. A strike landed clean through her guard, nine points of piercing damage followed by poison seeping into the wound, and the numbers did not favor her. Granit was in dire shape too, drawing healing from somewhere — a Healing Word cast with the desperation of someone who had counted the margins and found them insufficient. The healer tending to the party while tending to themselves, which is never a good sign.

The enemy had singled her out. There was something almost deliberate in it, the way the pressure kept returning to Rides the Wake — as though whatever intelligence directed the assault had identified the storm at the party's center and decided to put it down first. She fought through the first wave of it. She fought through the second. Vargr and Lyvriele pressed in, spending what they had, Granit's divine reserves running low. For a moment it seemed as though momentum might shift. It did not.

Eleven points of piercing damage, and Rides the Wake fell.

The figure that loomed over them in the aftermath was not a bandit, not a hired blade, not anything so small as that. The Grim Reaper stood in the space where she had been standing — vast and unhurried and absolute, the way only something that has never needed to hurry can be. The party stared up at it from various states of ruin. Granit was calling for death saving throws from somewhere to the left. The silence that followed the killing blow had the specific quality of silence that knows it has won.

The rest of the party survived by margins thin enough to read a name through. Lyvriele, as she had done before when the situation turned worst, remained on her feet — precise and cold where precision is the only thing left. Vargr had spent his Bardic Inspiration on someone who could no longer use it. The rebels somewhere in the lower city presumably had no idea that the people they had agreed to speak with were currently trying to determine whether they had enough bodies left to constitute a conversation. The encounter with Cael Duren at Pier Seven, with his composed threat and his unnamed patron, felt like a distant and almost quaint problem compared to the shape of what now occupied the room.

When the dust settled — if it settled — what remained was an inventory problem, a tactical problem, and a grief problem, in roughly that order. Supplies would need to be counted. A next target would need to be identified from among the city's tangled interests. The question of other islands, other nodes in the Shardsea of Governance's archipelago, hung unanswered in the air like smoke that hadn't decided where to drift. Somewhere in that calculation was also the question of whether Rides the Wake's death was final or merely the opening move in a negotiation with fate — but that was a question for a quieter moment, and this was not yet a quiet moment.
